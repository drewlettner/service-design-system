# Milwaukee Service UX — Look & Feel

## Experience Philosophy

Operational clarity over visual novelty.

Interfaces should feel:
- trustworthy
- calm
- structured
- industrial
- low-friction
- confidence-building

The system should guide users progressively without overwhelming them.

---

## Interaction Tone

- Direct, not robotic
- Helpful, not conversational
- Informational, not alarming
- Utility-first, not marketing-first

---

## Visual Characteristics

- Neutral surfaces
- Restrained use of color
- Blue = interaction/action
- Red = brand or critical state only
- Borders clarify structure, not decoration
- Cards represent service objects and states

---

## UX Patterns

- Progressive disclosure preferred over upfront complexity
- Optional paths should remain visually secondary
- Service escalation should occur contextually
- Important financial disclosures should feel informative, not punitive

---

## Mobile Philosophy

- Prioritize clarity over density
- Stack vertically when needed
- Preserve touch confidence
- Reduce accidental interaction risk
- Use bottom sheets for contextual actions

---

## Service Object Model

Products, repairs, calibrations, and estimates should behave as modular service objects that can scale across:
- eService
- OnePoint
- Cart/checkout
- Future workflows

---

## Avoid

- Overly loud warning states
- Decorative enterprise UI
- Over-styled cards
- Dense action clusters
- Hidden critical information
- Abrupt escalation patterns
